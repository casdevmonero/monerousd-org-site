Where the money goes

Cards pass your sale through five hands. This passes it through none.

A card payment travels through the customer's bank, the card network, your bank and a processor before it reaches you. Each takes a cut, each can hold your money, and for months afterwards the sale can be pulled back. A MoneroUSD payment is one step: the customer's wallet pays yours, and it is final.

  • The amount, the customer and your sales totals are hidden from everyone else
  • Settles to your own wallet; nobody can freeze it or close your account
  • Post-quantum cryptography end to end
Optional buyer protection

Escrow with no judge. Nothing to argue about.

Chargebacks exist because someone has to decide who is telling the truth, and that someone can be lied to. A protected purchase removes the decision. The price goes into a vault: an ordinary private payment that can leave in only three ways.

  • Receipt: the customer confirms they have the order, and you are paid at once
  • Agreement: you cancel or refund, and the money returns to the customer by itself
  • The clock: no delivery by the deadline you set, and the customer is refunded automatically

None of the three takes a claim as its input. There is no "I never got it" button, so there is nothing to lie to and nobody who judges. Protection is the shop's choice per sale, and costs the same $0.001 as a plain payment.

One Merchant tab

Four ways to sell.

Type what it is and the price, create the sale, share the link. The customer's wallet reads one sentence and pays.

Protected purchase

The price is held in escrow and released to you on delivery. Add a non-refundable deposit for made-to-order work, or a delivery bond to show you mean it.

In person

Tap to pay at the counter over Bluetooth. The charge is signed, so its amount can't be altered, and it settles in seconds.

Subscription

A recurring payment collected automatically each period. The subscriber can cancel at any time; nothing is ever pulled from their wallet.

Digital download

The customer pays and receives the file by itself: the payment that pays you is the same one that hands over the file's key.

At the doorstep

The parcel is on the porch. Nobody is home. Who proves it arrived?

This is the hard case of online selling: no handover, two people who can each say anything. The answer is a fact neither of them controls — the carrier's own delivery notice. Carriers sign their notification emails. Your wallet turns that signed "delivered" notice into a zero-knowledge proof, the customer's wallet checks it, and the payment is released. Nobody taps anything.

  • The parcel is pinned to the order before it ships: one tracking number, accepted by the customer's wallet
  • The carrier's email never leaves your machine — the customer's wallet learns only that the carrier signed "delivered" for the parcel it accepted
  • The carrier does nothing new and is never asked; nothing is added to the label

Delivery to the address the customer gave completes the sale. A parcel that comes back is a proof too: it refunds the customer and covers your postage both ways.

On your website

One Pay button. Told the moment the money lands.

Run the small checkout helper beside your wallet. Your shop asks it for an order, shows one Pay button, and receives a signed webhook when the payment arrives or enters escrow. There is no processor, no hosted service and no key issued by anyone.

  • A WooCommerce plugin, a Shopify connector, and a pay panel plus a five-call API for any other site
  • No-code option: a price list in the helper and a link you can paste anywhere
  • Wallet and shop talk over a hidden address, so a customer's network address never reaches you

Optional proof of income

Private by default. Provable when you choose.

Your revenue is invisible to everyone — and sometimes you need to show it: to a lender, a landlord, an accountant. One switch in Settings lets your wallet make a single proof of your income. It shows the total and how many payments make it up, and nothing else: not one payment's amount, not one customer.

  • Off by default, and off again every time the wallet opens
  • Tied to a reference you type, so a proof can't be reused somewhere else
  • Make it for one named verifier, or one anyone can check against their own node
Where it stands

Built, and being proven. Not yet for real money.

  • Built in the wallet: the Merchant tab, payment links, protected purchases, deposits, delivery bonds, subscriptions, downloads, pickup relays, the doorstep delivery proof and proof of income.
  • Passed on real nodes so far: a shop order, a protected order, a cancel-and-refund and a one-link sale. The deposit-and-bond step and the download step have not passed on real nodes yet.
  • Checkout: the helper passes its own tests; the WooCommerce plugin and Shopify connector have not been run against live shops yet.
  • The doorstep: the proof is built and checked between the two wallets. Each carrier's mail key is added only at release, after it has been confirmed from three independent sources, so no carrier is accepted in today's build.

What it does not do, in plain words

  • It never judges. A parcel stolen from the porch after delivery, or sent to an address the customer mistyped, is not refunded by the chain. Offer pickup at a carrier counter or a relay to close that.
  • At the doorstep, a customer running altered wallet software can refuse to confirm even against a valid proof. Until that proof is enforced on the chain itself, your cover in that case is the non-refundable deposit you set.
  • It does not tell you what is inside an unwatched box. Say in your offer who carries that, and the wallet shows it before the customer pays.
