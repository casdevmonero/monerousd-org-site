What the chain sees

A proof the rules were kept. Nothing else.

On a public chain, anyone can follow your money. On MoneroUSD, every transaction is a zero-knowledge proof: nodes check that no money was created and nothing was spent twice, without learning who paid whom, or how much.

  • No amounts, no addresses, no links between payments
  • Every block has the same shape, so real activity can't be counted
  • Your scan key never leaves your device — not even to your own node
How a private payment works

Your wallet proves it. The chain checks it.

Money lives in notes: sealed commitments only their owner can open. To pay, your wallet builds a zero-knowledge proof on your device. A one-time nullifier marks the old note spent, so the same note can never be spent twice — the spent set refuses it, the way Zcash does.

  • Post-quantum from the first byte: ML-KEM-768, ML-DSA-65, Poseidon2, WHIR, BLAKE3 and SHA-3
  • No elliptic curves anywhere — nothing to harvest now and decrypt later
  • Pay anyone by @username; every cost shown in dollars before you confirm
Consensus & scale

Parallel blocks, one order. The whole chain in one proof.

Blocks are produced in parallel and ordered the same way by every node (GHOSTDAG, as in Kaspa). Each block folds into a recursive proof, so a new node checks one proof and joins at the tip instead of replaying history.

  • Design targets: 500 ms blocks, 32 parallel blocks, a constant proof of at most 1.8 MB
  • Node state stays the same size as the chain grows — built so phones can run full nodes
  • One kind of node: it mines, verifies, proves and relays. No trusted servers.

Targets are tracked on real nodes and published as they are measured.

The wallet

Feels like a bank app. Works like cash.

A seed in one tap, balances read live from your own node, and every cost in dollars before you act. Payments are final: no chargebacks, no reversals, no account to freeze.

  • USDm, the dollar of MoneroUSD — every one minted against a BTC or XMR deposit, over-collateralized
  • Pay by @username, receive to one post-quantum address
  • Buy and sell with Bitcoin or Monero, earn, borrow, vote — all from the wallet

Built · testing on real nodes

Tap to pay · Bluetooth LE

Pay in person. No card network.

The merchant keys in the sale and the wallet advertises a signed charge over Bluetooth LE. The customer taps Pay in person, holds the phones together, sees the amount in dollars and approves. Done.

  • The charge is signed with ML-DSA, so its amount and destination can't be altered mid-tap
  • Range-gated to a few centimetres; an ML-KEM handshake means the radio only carries ciphertext
  • A 0.5% fee for merchants on the first-party POS, against 2–3% for cards

Built in the wallet · testing

Letter

Private messages, with money inside.

Letter is messaging built into the wallet. Each letter is sealed end to end with ML-KEM-768 and signed with ML-DSA-65. There's no phone number, no email and no server — your wallet is your login, and your history restores from your seed.

  • Send USDm inside a conversation
  • Groups, profiles and an optional fee to message you, against spam
  • Nothing about who talks to whom is published

Built · in the wallet

MoneroUSD Veil

Who talks to whom stays hidden too.

Encryption hides what you say; the network can still see who you talk to. The Veil is MoneroUSD's own mix network: your payment travels through three relays, each peeling one layer, inside constant cover traffic — so a real packet looks like noise.

  • Post-quantum links (ML-KEM-768); no IP addresses on the mix layer
  • Your payment leaves through a random node, never announced by your own
  • Private reads (PIR): a node serves data without learning which item you asked for

Sovereign

A browser for a web nobody can take down.

Publish a site from your wallet in three taps: pick a template, claim a .mud name, pay once in dollars. The site lives on the network's own nodes, owned by the key in your wallet. Sovereign checks every byte against the site's root on chain before it shows the page.

  • No host, no registrar, no certificate to renew — and nothing to rent each month
  • Sites run in a sandbox whose only network is the Veil: they can't leak your IP
  • Browsing is free; your history stays on your device

Introducing SAT

Satoshi (SAT): the reward asset of MoneroUSD.

SAT is mined by the nodes that secure the chain. It is the bond validators lock to guard custody and finality, it earns a share of network fees, and it trades directly against Bitcoin and Monero.

FAQ

Short answers

Is MoneroUSD live?

Not yet. It's in development and being tested on real nodes. A public testnet comes first; real funds only after the testnet, an outside audit, a four-week public soak and a guarded launch.

Is MoneroUSD part of Monero?

No. MoneroUSD is a separate project with its own chain and cryptography. It shares Monero's goal — private money — and its wallet trades directly with Monero (XMR) and Bitcoin (BTC).

What does "post-quantum" mean here?

Every key exchange, signature, commitment and proof uses schemes designed to resist quantum computers (ML-KEM, ML-DSA, Poseidon2, WHIR, BLAKE3, SHA-3). There are no elliptic curves, so there's nothing to record today and break later.

What is USDm?

The dollar of MoneroUSD. Every USDm is minted against a Bitcoin or Monero deposit and the system is over-collateralized. It's what you pay with.

What is SAT?

The reward asset: 21 million maximum, mined by the nodes that secure the chain, bonded by validators, and traded directly for BTC and XMR. Full tokenomics.
