SAT tokenomics

Satoshi (SAT): the reward asset of MoneroUSD.

USDm is what you pay with. SAT is what secures the network: it is mined by the nodes that run the chain, bonded by the validators that guard custody and finality, and paid a share of every fee.

21,000,000hard cap, enforced at every fold of the chain
19,000,000issued only by block rewards

Supply

Fixed, provable, and private where it should be.

No SAT can exist outside three doors: a block reward, the genesis set-aside, or an ordinary spend of SAT that already exists. Consensus refuses any reward that would push the issued total past the cap, and no other mint can create SAT.

The developer set-aside is disclosed. Up to 2,000,000 SAT (9.5% of the cap) is set aside at genesis. Genesis carries a fixed number of confidential SAT notes and one proof that their sum is at most 2,000,000. Whatever part of the 2M is not used is simply never created. You can verify the bound; nobody can read the exact amount — the same privacy every holder gets.

Emission

A Bitcoin-style halving, counted in blocks.

The reward for a block is R₀ >> (height ÷ 252,460,800): it starts at 0.0376296 SAT per block and halves every 252,460,800 blocks — four years at the network's 500 ms block-time target. Half of the 19M is issued in the first era, a quarter in the second, and so on, ending after about 21 eras.

The reward amount is public, because it follows from the schedule. Who received it is private: each reward lands as a confidential note only the miner's wallet can find, and every later transfer hides amount, sender and receiver.

Utility

What SAT does.

Secures the chain

Validators bond SAT — only SAT — to guard Bitcoin and Monero custody and to finalize blocks. By design, a validator that signs a theft loses its bond to the victims.

Earns fees

A quarter of every network fee buys SAT for bonders. Any holder can delegate SAT to a validator in one tap; holding stays the default.

Rewards mining

The nodes that produce blocks earn SAT, and later a share of fees from the miner pool, so security doesn't end when rewards do.

Trades directly

SAT/BTC and SAT/XMR pools let anyone buy or sell at any time and be paid in BTC or XMR — no order book, no waiting.

Fees

Every fee goes back to the network.

Each transaction pays a flat $0.001 network fee in USDm. It's the same for everyone, so the fee itself reveals nothing about the amount. Fees are pooled in chain state, paid out quarterly and split four ways. There is no founder share in protocol fees.

  • 40% to USDm stakers
  • 25% buys SAT for validators' bonds
  • 25% to the surplus buffer that backs USDm
  • 10% to the miner pool — rising to 15% in era 2 and 20% from era 3, taken from the stakers' share

Apps may add their own interface fee, shown in dollars before you act. The first-party wallet charges 0.25% on swaps, 0.10% to cash in or out, and 0.5% to merchants on its POS. Doing the same thing through another front end pays only the network fee.

Trading SAT

Direct pools, private batches.

At launch, SAT opens at $1 in two direct pools: SAT/BTC and SAT/XMR. Buy SAT by depositing Bitcoin or Monero; sell it and be paid in whichever coin you choose. Orders are cleared in sealed batches: the chain publishes one clearing price per pair per batch, and nothing else — no volume, no depth, no positions, no fills.

Each order can carry a maximum price to protect the buyer; it bounds that one order, never the market.

SAT is part of the MoneroUSD design and is being tested on real nodes. It is not available to buy today, and nothing on this site is financial advice. Final parameters are fixed in the launch release.
